# Snap Vanish

Snap your fingers on a video call and you turn to dust and blow away, leaving an empty room where you were sitting. Your mic mutes at the same moment. Snap again and the dust flies back, you reassemble, and your mic unmutes.

Works in Chrome (and other Chromium browsers such as Edge and Brave) with Google Meet, Microsoft Teams and Zoom in the browser.

## Install

1. Download the latest `snap-vanish-x.y.z.zip` from [Releases](https://github.com/hunainx/snap-vanish/releases) and unzip it, or clone this repo. You get a folder called `snap-vanish`.
2. In Chrome, open `chrome://extensions`.
3. Turn on **Developer mode** (top right).
4. Click **Load unpacked** and choose the `snap-vanish` folder (the one that contains `manifest.json`).
5. Pin Snap Vanish from the puzzle-piece menu so its icon is always in the toolbar.

Keep the folder where it is afterwards; Chrome loads the extension from it.

## Before your first meeting

Click the toolbar icon, then **Practise without a meeting**. Turn on your camera and mic and try a few snaps. The *Snap check* panel lights up as it sees your fingers set, the snap motion and the click sound, so you can find the right sensitivity.

## Using it in a meeting

1. Make sure Snap Vanish is switched on (toolbar popup), then join the meeting with your camera on. Already in a call? Turn your camera off and on once.
2. Sit still for a few seconds, then lean left and right once. Every part of the wall the camera sees behind you is used as the real background. Whatever it never sees is filled in by an on-device AI model. The popup shows how much of the room has been learned and says **Ready** at 80%. You can snap earlier, but the AI then has more to guess.
3. **Snap** to vanish. **Snap again** to come back.

Backups if you don't want to snap:

- **Snap now** button in the toolbar popup
- Keyboard shortcut **Alt+Shift+S** (change it at `chrome://extensions/shortcuts`)

The toolbar badge shows **ON** while it is handling your camera and **GONE** while you have vanished.

## Settings (toolbar popup)

| Setting | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| On/off switch | Off means your camera and mic are passed to the meeting untouched. |
| Sensitivity | Strict needs a crisp, clear snap. Easy accepts softer, slower snaps. |
| Confirm with the snap's click sound | The hand-tracking gesture must be backed up by the snap's click in your mic. Turn off if you can't snap loudly; the gesture check then gets stricter. |
| Also click the meeting's mute button | Besides silencing your mic itself, it clicks the meeting's own mute button so others see the muted icon. |
| Duration | How long the dust takes, 1.5 to 8 seconds. |
| Direction | Which way the dust blows, as other people see you: left, right, up or scatter. |
| Also clear my shadow off the wall (beta) | Looks for your shadow on the wall right around you and fills it in too while you're gone. Off by default: it can miss soft or distant shadows. |
| Re-learn the room | Forget the learned background. Use it after you move the camera. |

## How it works

- **Camera**: when the meeting asks for your camera, it receives a processed copy instead. Until you snap, frames pass straight through unchanged.
- **Snap detection**: Google's MediaPipe hand tracker follows 21 points on each hand in 3D. A snap is the thumb pressed against the middle fingertip, followed within a fraction of a second by the middle finger slamming down to the palm while the thumb flies away. Waving, typing, pointing, pinching and making a fist don't match. The mic then confirms it: a snap is a very short, sharp click, unlike speech.
- **Empty room**: a person segmentation model separates you from the background. While you are visible, the extension builds a clean picture of the room from pixels that have been steadily visible behind you. The parts it has never seen (usually right behind your body) are generated by MI-GAN, an inpainting model, from the surrounding room.
- **Lighting**: it keeps track of how bright the room is right now compared with when each part of it was learned, and adjusts the learned room and the AI fill to match, so the empty spot doesn't stand out when a light goes on or your camera changes exposure.
- **Dust**: a GPU particle system breaks your image into a few hundred thousand grains of different sizes that streak away in the wind, glow briefly at the edge, and fade to ash.
- **Mic**: your microphone track is silenced at the source, and the meeting's mute button is pressed if that setting is on. On return, it only unmutes what it muted.

## Privacy

Everything runs on your computer. The models and runtimes are bundled inside the extension, and nothing it does sends video, audio or images over the network. The only permission it asks for is `storage` (to remember your settings). It runs only on Google Meet, Teams and Zoom pages.

## Troubleshooting

- **"Waiting for your camera"**: switch Snap Vanish on, then turn your camera off and on in the meeting (or reload the meeting tab).
- **Snaps not detected**: open the practice page, keep your hand clearly in view at chest height, palm roughly facing the camera. Raise the sensitivity, or untick the click-sound check if your snap is quiet.
- **Triggers by accident**: lower the sensitivity and keep the click-sound check on.
- **Bits of you show while "gone"**: you moved a lot right before snapping, or the room hasn't been learned yet. Lean left and right once at the start of the call so it can see behind you, and wait for **Ready**.
- **A faint outline of you stays where you sat**: that is the AI-filled part. The more of the room it has seen (80% or more), the less it shows.
- **Room looks wrong after moving the laptop**: click **Re-learn the room**.
- **It feels slow**: the first minute includes loading the models. Close other heavy tabs. The extension picks GPU or CPU automatically, whichever is faster on your machine.

## Limits

- Zoom support is for the browser client (`app.zoom.us`), not the Zoom desktop app. Teams and Meet must also be used in the browser.
- The AI fill invents what it has never seen. A plain wall looks perfect; a busy bookshelf directly behind you may look smudged, and if it never saw much of the room you may see a faint outline where you sat.
- Others in the frame may also disappear, because they're people too.

## Development

There is no build step: the repo folder is the extension. Load it with **Load unpacked**, edit, then press the reload icon on its card in `chrome://extensions`.

- `content/inject.js` — camera/mic interception, snap and click detectors, WebGL renderer (room, fill, dust)
- `content/bridge.js` — link between the page and the extension
- `engine/` — on-device models (MediaPipe hand tracking and segmentation, MI-GAN inpainting) in an extension iframe and worker
- `popup/`, `try/` — toolbar popup and practice page
- `tests/detector.test.mjs` — snap/click detector tests: `node tests/detector.test.mjs` (Node 20+)

Issues and pull requests are welcome. Please keep everything on-device: no network calls, no analytics.
